TITLE
=====
Compression Ratio Is Not a Serving Metric: A Measurement Study of KV-Cache Compression for Long-Context LLM Serving

ABSTRACT
========
Long-context LLM serving is bounded by the key–value (KV) cache, and a large literature compresses it. Almost all of that literature summarises a method to a prospective adopter with two numbers: a compression ratio and a task score. A serving system consumes neither; it consumes prefill latency, decode throughput and resident device memory. We ask whether the two reported numbers predict the three consumed ones, and measure that in our study they do not.

Across 3,150 paired query executions — each compressed run matched to an uncompressed run of the same model on the identical prompt — spanning seven compression methods, three open-weight models, five long-context tasks and contexts from 2K to 128K tokens, we find: (i) at an identical nominal budget of 4 bits per KV element, with realised payloads within 0.19 bits of one another, task accuracy ranges from 2.5% to 47.3% on one model, an 18.7× spread; (ii) two methods matched to within 0.55 effective bits per element and 2.0 accuracy points, on the same model and the same prompts, differ by 30.3× in time-to-first-token and 3.46× in peak VRAM; and (iii) over 36 operating points we detect no rank relationship between the memory a representation saves and the hardware efficiency it realises (ρ = +0.04, p = 0.84), nor between its effective footprint and its prefill cost (ρ = +0.01, p = 0.98). The reported footprint is itself systematically understated: charging a representation for the metadata that makes it decodable adds 6–23% depending on method family, and reorders the resulting ranking.

We describe TriAxis, the measurement methodology these findings required: query-level pairing against a dense reference, an effective-bits accounting that charges for mandatory metadata, and a report that keeps representation utility and measured serving efficiency on separate axes instead of merging them. We give per-method provenance, state for every method whether its saving was physically realised or analytically accounted, and scope each conclusion to the implementations and hardware measured. The per-query records and the scripts that regenerate every number and figure are released.
